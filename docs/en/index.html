Thinking about Asia‑Pacific from Peru

The Peruvian Network for Asia-Pacific Studies (REDAP) is an inter-university cooperation initiative that strengthens study, research and academic ties between Peru and the countries of Asia-Pacific. Founded in 1997, it brings together 16 public and private institutions.

REDAP objectives

Choose an objective to read it in full.

Foster regional and international academic cooperation

Promote research, exchange and cooperation among academic institutions in Peru and in the countries of Asia-Pacific. This objective seeks to strengthen ties among universities, study centers and knowledge networks, encouraging their participation in the main academic and scientific dialogue mechanisms in the Asian region.

Connect academia, business and the State

Encourage interaction among the academic sector, the business sector and government institutions to strengthen Peru’s integration into Asia-Pacific. REDAP promotes spaces for dialogue, evidence-based policy and joint projects that bring together science, technology and economic diplomacy.

Deepen knowledge of Asia-Pacific

Improve understanding of the economic, social, political and cultural realities of the countries that make up the Asia-Pacific region. REDAP drives research, publications and training spaces that make it possible to analyze regional transformations and their impact on Peru and Latin America.

Contribute to Peru’s strategic positioning in the region

Promote Peru’s active participation in mechanisms such as APEC, strengthening the role of academic knowledge in public policy design, economic cooperation and engagement with key international actors. REDAP acts as a bridge between university research and the country’s strategic challenges in Asia-Pacific.

Mission and vision

Consolidate an inter-institutional academic space that promotes study, research, cooperation and specialized training on Asia-Pacific, bringing Peruvian universities together around a common agenda of international engagement, scientific development and the strategic projection of Peru in the region.
