Who coordinated the network

The coordination passes from one member institution to another. These are the ones recorded in the network’s archive, which is still being completed.

2013, National University of Piura

In 2013 the network’s coordination was led by the National University of Piura.

2019–2020, Cayetano Heredia Peruvian University

It coordinated the network in 2019 and until November 2020, when it handed the coordination over to the University of the Pacific.

2020–2021, University of the Pacific

It received the coordination on November 25, 2020 and handed it over to the University of Lima in December 2021.

2021–2023, University of Lima

It coordinated the network from December 2021 until February 2023, when the coordination passed to ESAN University.

2023, ESAN University

It received the coordination on February 16, 2023.

2025, National University of San Marcos

In 2025 the coordination was at the San Marcos Asian Studies Center (CEAS). During that period, the network’s website was implemented on Google Sites.

2026, National Agrarian University La Molina

In 2026, until August, the coordination was at the National Agrarian University La Molina.

2026–2027, Pontifical Catholic University of Peru

Since September 2026, PUCP has coordinated the network for one year.

1997

Founding of REDAP

The Peruvian Network for Asia-Pacific Studies (REDAP) was established through a cooperation agreement signed in 1997 by prestigious Peruvian universities:

  • Pontifical Catholic University of Peru (PUCP)
  • Cayetano Heredia Peruvian University (UPCH)
  • University of the Pacific (UP)
  • University of Lima (UL)
  • ESAN University (ESAN)
  • National Agrarian University La Molina (UNALM)
  • National University of Engineering (UNI)

These institutions recognized the need to establish an inter-institutional academic platform that strengthens ties between Peru and the Asia-Pacific region through teaching, research and cooperation.

1998

Accession of the Diplomatic Academy of Peru

The Diplomatic Academy of Peru “Javier Pérez de Cuéllar,” the institution that trains the country’s future diplomats, joined REDAP in 1998, in recognition of the growing strategic importance of the Asia-Pacific region in Peruvian foreign policy.

Its accession strengthened the multidisciplinary nature of the network by integrating an entity specialized in international relations, foreign policy and public diplomacy. Since joining, the Academy has actively contributed to developing academic spaces aimed at training professionals with specialized knowledge of Asia-Pacific, as well as to promoting dialogue among academia, the State and international organizations.

2005

New universities join REDAP

In 2005, REDAP broadened its institutional base with the addition of two universities of great importance in the national academic landscape: the National University of San Marcos (UNMSM) and the University of Piura.

The National University of San Marcos joined the network with the aim of strengthening research and debate on Asia-Pacific. Its participation has helped consolidate a national perspective on the Asian region, with an emphasis on the international reach of knowledge generated at the public university.

For its part, the University of Piura brought a regional dimension by adding, from the north of the country, a solid track record in economics and the humanities. Its accession responded to the interest in integrating applied approaches and promoting institutional relations with Asian universities, as well as to a commitment to decentralize academic analysis of Asia-Pacific in Peru.

2008

First APEC study centers conference in Peru and new universities join

In 2008, REDAP continued its expansion with the addition of the National University of Tumbes and the Peruvian University of Applied Sciences (UPC).

This growth coincided with a key moment on the country’s international agenda: Peru’s presidency of the APEC forum in 2008, the year in which the APEC Study Centers Consortium (ASCC) conference was organized in Peru for the first time. REDAP supported this context academically, creating spaces for reflection and analysis from the university on the challenges of economic and political integration in Asia-Pacific.
